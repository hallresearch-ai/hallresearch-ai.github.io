What is an AI Research Guide?

The HallResearch.ai Library is intentionally broad. It collects useful resources across AI governance, public policy, law, institutional practice, incidents, accountability, assessment, evaluation, auditing, testing, tooling, documentation, education, and related areas.

AI Research Guides provide a different way into that material.

Instead of asking, What resources are available?, a guide starts with a more focused question:

What should I read, compare, and ask if I want to understand this problem?

Each completed guide is intended to include:

  • A curated source corpus selected from across the HallResearch.ai Library
  • A short orientation explaining the problem and the scope of the collection
  • Questions to try for researchers, practitioners, students, policymakers, and other users
  • A source manifest documenting what is included
  • An interactive notebook, where platform access permits
  • A review date and changelog so readers can see when the collection was last checked or materially revised

The aim is not comprehensiveness for its own sake. A Research Guide is a deliberately bounded research environment: enough strong and complementary material to help someone get oriented, compare perspectives, identify tensions, and pursue a question further.

How the guides connect to the Library

AI Research Guides are built from the two main branches of the HallResearch.ai Library.

AI Governance & Policy Resources

Resources related to AI governance, public policy, law, institutional guidance, incidents, accountability, labor, civil-society critique, and the social consequences of AI deployment.

AI Assessment & Implementation Resources

Resources related to responsible machine-learning assessment, implementation practice, evaluation, auditing, testing, tooling, documentation, and education.

A Research Guide may draw from either or both branches. The Library remains the larger resource collection; the guides provide curated pathways through it.

What is coming next?

Our first goal is deliberately modest: publish a small set of strong guides before expanding the collection.

  • ☑ Establish the AI Research Guides repository
  • ☑ Select the initial public guide collection
  • ☑ Curate the underlying source corpora
  • ☐ Finalize titles and guide metadata
  • ☐ Create individual guide pages
  • ☐ Add complete source manifests
  • ☐ Add suggested research questions
  • ☐ Establish public interactive-notebook access
  • ☐ Link Research Guides from the two main Library repositories
  • ☐ Begin adding additional guides based on usefulness and demand

Tip. Why publish the catalog before everything is finished?
We want the repository itself to make the direction of the project visible. The guides listed here are already being curated; public interfaces, documentation, and stable access links are the pieces still being assembled.

Design principles

Curated, not dumped

A guide should contain enough material to support serious inquiry without simply reproducing an entire Library category.

Source-grounded

The value of a guide comes from the quality and composition of its sources. Interactive tools are interfaces to the corpus, not substitutes for it.

Public by default

Where rights and platform constraints allow, the goal is to make guide pages, source manifests, and interactive research environments openly accessible.

Platform-independent

HallResearch.ai maintains the guide title, source manifest, scope, orientation material, and revision history here. Interactive notebooks may currently be hosted using third-party tools, but no individual platform is the authoritative record of a guide.

Maintained over time

Completed guides will include review dates and changelogs. When a source is replaced, a guide materially changes, or a better resource becomes available, the GitHub record can document that change.

Future guides

The initial six are only the starting point. Existing HallResearch.ai collections may support future guides on topics including:

  • university AI policies and teaching guidance
  • critical and responsible perspectives on AI
  • regional and national AI policy
  • major institutional AI frameworks
  • sector-specific governance
  • AI incidents and accountability
  • emerging questions identified through continued Library curation

We expect the collection to grow selectively rather than on a fixed schedule.
